Step 0 Calculator

Prep estimates only. No DSP values are written at Step 0. Delays and levels here are first-pass targets to verify during live calibration. Opens with the example rig loaded.

How to use this app

Version . Step 0 is prep only: it estimates delays, levels and coverage so you arrive at live calibration with targets. It never produces DSP values.

1. Fill in what you know

  1. Open the Inputs side. Leave blank anything you do not know: blanks use industry-standard defaults, listed under "Defaults applied" in Results.
  2. Enter the venue (room size, ceiling, wall materials), the temperature at the mix position, the target SPL and the furthest audience distance.
  3. Fill in each cluster: FOH-L and FOH-R tops, Center, Subs and Delay. Quantity starts at 1: raise it until the Mains reach your target (the line under Clusters shows how far off you are). A Center, Sub or Delay cluster with nothing entered but the quantity is left out of the rig.
  4. For Subs, set the configuration (L/R stacked with FOH, or mono center) and the pattern (omni, cardioid or end-fire).
  5. Load example shows a worked rig. Clear all starts blank.

2. Use Lookup to save typing

  1. Type the venue name and city at the top. Type a speaker model inside each cluster that has one.
  2. Paste a search result into a cluster: open Paste spec text or load a file under any cluster, paste the specs from a web page or data sheet (or load a .txt, .csv, .md, .json or .html file), and press Read specs. It picks out RMS or continuous watts, sensitivity, max SPL, coverage and (for subs) the pattern, and shows the matched lines under Results so you can check them. PDFs cannot be read here: copy the spec table out of the PDF and paste it.
  3. One lookup entry: fill the four lines in the Lookup entry box: Venue: a name and city or a description such as covered basketball court, Valenzuela City; Sound system: for example 2x Mackie Thump212XT tops + 2x Mackie Thump118S subs, or leave it blank to ask the AI for the installed system; Application: Full range music, Acoustic or Speech only; Delay approach: Ideal or Budget. The Application and Delay approach lines set the selectors, and the selectors update the lines.
  4. Ask AI to look up the venue and sound system: reads the entry, fills every speaker it finds in the built-in table straight away (quantity and manufacturer specs, offline, no extra button), then copies a request for the venue and anything not in the table and opens your AI chat. Send it, copy the AI's whole reply, come back and press Paste AI reply and fill. Models in the built-in table keep their data sheet specs, not the AI's numbers. Undo import reverts everything.
  5. Job log: when every item in the CalTemp sign-off is ticked, Save signed-off job to the log stores the inputs and the Step 0 estimates. Each saved job then needs a calibration report: the final delays in the DSP, the measured front, middle and back SPL, the practical adjustments and the deviations. The log compares predicted with measured (delays in ms, level drops against the front row in dB) and can be downloaded as JSON or CSV or imported on another device. It is stored in this browser only, so download a copy.
  6. Each cluster has its own Ask AI about this speaker button. If the model is in the built-in table it fills instantly and offline. If not, it copies a request and opens your AI chat the same way. Choose Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini under AI chat to open for lookups. Anything not found stays blank so you can type it. Filled fields get a blue edge: they are unverified until you confirm them.
  7. No AI key is needed or stored. The page cannot call an AI itself, so the AI step always happens in your own AI chat. If your browser blocks reading the clipboard, paste the reply into Import lookup data and press Fill fields.
  8. Undo import restores your values from before the last lookup. Always check the sources shown under Results.

Speaker models (works offline)

  • Type or pick a model in a cluster (for example Yamaha DXR12) and press its lookup button. If the model is in the built-in table, the cluster fills instantly from the manufacturer data sheet with no internet. Only models not in the table go to the AI.
  • Blank means the manufacturer does not publish it. Programme watts are shown halved and marked derived. If only a continuous SPL is published, peak is continuous + 3 dB and marked derived. The source link and any derived values appear in the lookup details.
  • L-Acoustics, d&b and Meyer publish peak SPL measured with a high crest factor signal. The app's peak minus 3 dB rule overstates continuous level for those, and the lookup says so.
  • Save this model to the list (in each cluster) keeps the make, model and the specs currently in that cluster on this device. Next time, the lookup button fills them offline. They are marked unverified. Use Copy my models in the Database tab to send them for checking and adding to the shared list.
  • Alignment reference: pick which cluster is the zero point under Clusters, or leave it on Auto. The cluster farthest from the listening position (the RTA mic) gets zero delay and every other cluster, including the Mains, is delayed to match. Usually that is the Mains. When the Mains sit ahead of the subs, set the subs behind the Mains (Direction: Behind) and the subs become the zero point.
  • Modeling rule: every cluster is a point source with one timing. Doubling the boxes in a cluster adds 3 dB by power sum.

Brands reference (works offline)

  • The Database tab is a built-in list of loudspeaker and amplifier brands, from vintage to current and OEM drivers and amplifier modules. Search it, or filter by type, segment and status.
  • The speaker model boxes in Clusters suggest brand names as you type.
  • Update database downloads newer brands and models from the repository when you are online and keeps it in this browser. Use built-in copy goes back. The list is a reference, not a spec sheet, and ownership and status change over time.

3. Read the results

  • Delay table: propagation delay per cluster using c = 331 + 0.6 × temperature. These are first guesses to verify live.
  • Levels: estimated SPL per cluster, combined Mains level, and level at the furthest audience point against your target.
  • Flags: red needs attention before calibration, amber is worth checking, blue is information.
  • Copy summary puts the whole result on your clipboard for notes or messages.

4. Go to CalTemp

CalTemp turns the result into a step-by-step live calibration: SPL and headroom mapping, then time and phase alignment, then live transfer function tuning, then playback and coverage verification. No EQ before SPL and time alignment are confirmed, and never boost a null.

Good to know

  • Everything is a free-field estimate. Real rooms, especially reflective ones, will differ. Power and electrical draw are not checked.
  • Your inputs and settings are saved in this browser on this computer only.
  • Coverage reach geometry is an extension of the Scrolls, not a Scrolls formula. Flag anything that should change in AudioNinjaScrolls.

Updates

When this computer is online the app checks for a newer version and shows a banner at the top with a Download button. A web page cannot replace its own file, so download the new file and open it instead of this one. Your saved settings carry over in the same browser.

Application

Ideal design puts the next cluster at one doubling of distance. Budget accepts up to 10 dB of drop for venues that cannot afford delay clusters, such as small churches. It only sets the default when the Delay L distance is blank.

Clusters

Type a speaker model in each cluster and use its look-up button to fill that cluster's specs. Anything the lookup cannot find stays blank for you to type. Quantity starts at 1: raise it until the Mains reach your target. A Center, Sub or Delay cluster with only the quantity set is left out of the rig; set a quantity of 0 to remove FOH-L or FOH-R. Distance is how far ahead of (or behind) the Mains reference the cluster sits. Give either sensitivity with RMS watts, or the rated peak SPL at 1 m.

Time alignment is done from one listening position (the RTA mic). The cluster farthest from it gets zero delay and the others are delayed to match. Usually that is the Mains. If the Mains sit ahead of the subs, the subs are the zero point and the Mains get the delay. "Distance ahead of Mains" is measured toward the audience along the throw.

Coverage and aim

Leave blank to use industry-standard assumptions. The coverage angles come from the FOH-L tops cluster above. Trim, spread and front row distance are placement decisions: a lookup only fills them if a rider states them.
